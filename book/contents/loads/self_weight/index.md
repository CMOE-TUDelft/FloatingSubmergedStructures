# Self Weight

The self weight of a structure is the total force resulting from its own mass and any permanent components attached to it. The self weight plays a central role in floating and submerged systems because it has a direct impact on the other static loads. 

:::{card} Definition
For a body of mass _m_ the self weight $W$ is defined as:

$$
W = mg
$$

Where:
*   $m$ = mass of the structure (kg),
*   $g$ = gravitational acceleration (≈ 9.81 m/s²).
:::

## Key Characteristics
1. **Point of Application:** The self weight acts vertically downward through the **Center of Gravity (G)**. The location of G depends on the mass distribution of the structure and is critical for stability analysis.
2. **Dead Load:** Self weight is a permanent, static load. Unlike variable loads (e.g. traffic) or environmental loads, it remains constant.
3. **Impact on Hydrostatics:** The magnitude of the self weight determines the required buoyancy. For a free-floating structure, the self weight directly dictates the draft (submerged depth). However, in practical applications, vertical forces from mooring systems or other connections can also pull the structure down or lift it up, changing the final equilibrium depth.
